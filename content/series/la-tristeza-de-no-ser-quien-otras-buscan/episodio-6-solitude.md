It's easier said than done.

Solitude is a state we choose to be in, not a casualty or accidental.

I don't really think this will be anything about solitude, but about loneliness. Since I don't really think I got anything out of the time I spent by myself. It almost feels like it's the old me talking again, thinking about ways to die alone, ways to live fast and die young.

It's either the confusion or not being able to tell for how long I chose to stay by myself. Wondering to myself: what was it really that you were trying to get out of it? Should I go for it again? What about those distractions? I know solitude was something I really wanted, I just didn't know how to practice it.

I guess that my challenge was really having extended periods, long days spent by myself getting to know myself. The main necessity was to view the real world from my perspective, the hardest parts of it was getting over obstacles not to fight any of them.

I believe people choose solitude for different reasons. My own reason was to understand creativity, thoughtful ideas, and originality. The best results I had from choosing solitude from time to time was that I was really able to subvocalize clearly in my head where all of this was beginning from. It was a whole process of learning, understanding and explanation that resulted in creation. This eventually led to find easier ways of communicating these thoughts to myself, slowly have more to define, ideate, build, and test. In solitude it is much easier to visualize *new* solutions to problems.
