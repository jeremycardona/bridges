If I had to give a quick definition about what these two words mean in my own words (might be wrong):
"Close and nearby God, an image of His qualities and traits"

This just brings me back to that first time learning about humanities in the classroom. I just did not know what to expect. I was just being present, being me, and being active. Having to wonder about how a dome is an incredible architectonic element of a structure. Mentally trying to figure out how to have one resist weights like the one of heavy lights. Not only that but, having the same architect build it, and having him paint it, making decorations, and pieces of art in the ceiling that would require so much divinity, virtue and talent. I guess I have been forever stuck in that moment because I thought it could be me. It really kept me in the tip of the iceberg for the longest of my young adulthood, because all I was taught was a little bit about renascence.

---
That eventually led me to try more modern things: user experience, cognitive psychology, giving computer science a break, design, pattern design, design patterns, starting computer science again from a discrete mathematics perspective, trying to be more like the teacher I wanted and the student I am (plainly a self-taught).

Realizing that at some point I would need to bridge the gap of the STEM field with 'just art'.

Most of the conclusions I after all this time is that I would be foundationally wrong without God.
Not only that but I am only human, and imperfection is inherited.

Fundamentals are always important, specially when and where we are trying to build.
What if in the modern world it starts differently in the way we build 'elementary', 'basic', 'intermediate', 'superior', 'higher', ..., all the way to the 'dome'?
And it feels like lying, what if primitive, and native are the first two levels? Elementary doesn't even feel like the first or the third, more like being stuck in the gap of native, *kinder*, and elementary. 

It gets absurd when thinking that in every different level there is a differentiated taste of love for everything.
